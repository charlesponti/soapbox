How To Think About Money

Most people chase money and let it dictate their choices. The alternative is alignment — choose work that energizes you and let the money follow.

  • economics
  • money
  • career
  • values
  • consumption

The woman at the coffee shop ordered her usual latte, then checked her phone for her bank balance before tapping her card. The barista handed her the drink. She walked to her car—a Mercedes with monthly payments that consume a third of her take-home pay. She drives forty minutes each way to a job she tolerates because it pays well. The coffee was $6. The commute costs her three hours daily. The job will never love her back.

This is how most people think about money. They chase it. They let it dictate their choices. They build their lives around earning more of it, then spend it on things that impress people they don’t even like.

The obsession with earning drives people into work they hate, relationships they endure, and lives they wouldn’t choose if money weren’t the primary consideration. We hit ceilings. We wait for promotions. We trade time for dollars until we realize we’re trading our lives for a paycheck that never feels sufficient.

Desire scales infinitely. Earn $100,000, want $200,000. Reach $1 million, need $10 million. The hunger grows with feeding. This is the system itself, not a bug in it. Consumer capitalism requires perpetual dissatisfaction to function.

The alternative requires a fundamental shift. Stop chasing money. Start chasing alignment.

Choose work that energizes you, even if the initial pay disappoints. Many fields reward passion over time in ways that salary negotiations never will. The artist who masters their craft eventually commands premium prices. The entrepreneur who solves real problems builds wealth that employees never access. The craftsperson who pursues excellence finds customers willing to pay for quality.

This approach demands patience. It requires believing that value creation eventually generates value capture. It means making decisions based on fulfillment rather than immediate financial gain.

The woman at the coffee shop could have chosen differently. She could have found work closer to home. She could have chosen a profession she actually enjoyed. She could have bought a car that served her needs rather than her insecurities.

Instead, she built a life around earning money to pay for a lifestyle designed to impress people whose opinions shouldn’t matter. Money became her master rather than her tool.

Choose work that aligns with your values. Let the money follow.
